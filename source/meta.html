About this book

This textbook takes a reader with no system-design background to a working understanding of production architecture. Each chapter explains a concept from a simple definition, shows why it exists, walks through how it works, and finishes with failure modes, trade-offs, interview questions and exercises.

How the book is built

The syllabus comes from the 51-page handwritten notebook System Design Primer — Complete Handwritten Study Notes. Every chapter opening states which part is derived from that notebook and which material was added to reach professional depth. Added material inside a chapter is boxed as Supplemental material. The final appendix maps every notebook topic to its chapter.

Reading the boxes

BoxMeaning
Learning objectivesWhat you can do after the chapter
Everyday analogyA non-software picture of the idea
Worked exampleNumbers or code worked through step by step
Common mistakeA frequent error and why it hurts
ClarificationA point where the original notes were compressed or imprecise
Interview questionsTypical questions, with explained answers
Supplemental materialAdded beyond the notebook

A note on numbers

Capacity figures in worked designs are assumptions chosen for teaching, not measurements. Always replace them with measurements from your own system. Latency figures are rough orders of magnitude and change with hardware.
